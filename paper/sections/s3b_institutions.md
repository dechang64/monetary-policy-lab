## 3.0 Institutional background: the statement as an instrument

The press release's role changed four times in ways that matter for what a text measure can capture. In **February 1994** the FOMC began announcing its decision immediately — before that, markets inferred the move from the open-market desk's operations. In **2000–2002** the post-meeting statement acquired the balance-of-risks paragraph, giving the committee a forward-looking sentence whose words, not actions, were the news. In **2005** the statement was restructured around the committee's assessment of the economy, deliberately shortening the action description — the modern text-first form. In **August 2011** the committee adopted calendar-based forward guidance ("at least through mid-2013"), making *words about the path* the operative instrument at the zero lower bound; the August 2020 framework review (flexible average inflation targeting) formalized state-contingent guidance again.

Three of our regimes follow this institutional sequence: the forward-guidance window (December 2008 – December 2015) is the era in which the statement, not the instrument, carried the marginal news; the 2020–2022 zero-rate window is the crisis-template era; the post-2022 cycle re-tightens with dot-plot and "sufficiently restrictive" language. The paradigm-shift claims of Section 7 — dictionary failure on Operation Twist, QE3, calendar guidance, the 2020 templates, and the 2022 acceleration — are therefore not statistical artifacts: each flagged statement sits on an institutional boundary that the committee itself defined.
