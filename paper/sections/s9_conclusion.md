# 9. Conclusion

We asked how Federal Reserve communication reaches asset prices and household portfolios, and answered with an identification — the market's inversion rule — rather than a new text score. The answer has three parts. On the announcement day, both the conventional and the information components of policy news move prices with the Jarociński-Karadi sign structure, and the statement's tone carries incremental price information precisely in the forward-guidance era — hawks lift yields and sink valuations at once, and the text survives a horse race against both shocks. Monthly flows ignore the words entirely: the null holds for the shocks, for tone, and for an audited LLM measure, at every window we can construct. What flows do respond to is the *risk content* of the information — a de-risking ladder on hawkish information, sign-asymmetric, with a zero-rate-regime exception that reveals the conventional channel operating through rescue capacity.

For the communication-paradigm literature, the measurement chapter delivers a caution with teeth: the dictionary does not mis-read paradigm-shift statements, it fails to differentiate them (65× resolution loss on the eleven episodes that define the shifts), so dictionary-based tone findings on exactly those episodes are under-identified. The LLM-based diagnostic — audited, cross-vendor confirmed, and positioned as a measurement tool rather than a competitor — is the repair we offer.

**Limitations.** Flow aggregation is monthly and class-level; the window structure, however carefully bounded, cannot separate intra-month timing. The text measures cover statements only — minutes and speeches are outside scope. The LLM layer inherits vendor dependence; the committee rule and the audit protocol bound, but do not eliminate, that dependence.

**Policy reading.** If prices clear the words and portfolios follow the risk, then central-bank communication works on household portfolios *through repricing*, not through persuasion — and communication regimes that shift the paradigm (forward guidance, structural operations) are precisely where text-measurement tools must be re-validated before their scores are interpreted.
