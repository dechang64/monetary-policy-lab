## 1.1 Related literature

**Surprise decomposition.** Kuttner (2001) established the window-measurement principle: extract policy news from asset prices in a narrow announcement window. Gürkaynak, Sack and Swanson (2005) decomposed that news into a target factor and a path factor from futures prices. Jarociński and Karadi (2020) added the sign-restriction dimension that this paper builds on — distinguishing news about actions from news about the central bank's information — and Coibion and Gorodnichenko (2015) supplied the information-friction discipline: disagreement and forecast revisions move with exactly the kind of central-bank information shock our CBI component isolates. Bauer and Swanson (2023) tightened the RE discipline — the market's price response is the policy surprise up to risk correction — which is the inversion rule we state as a one-line classification. Our contribution relative to this line is the object: statements (all 161, 2006–2026), decomposed by the rule, traced into *portfolios* rather than prices alone.

**Statement text and tone.** Loughran and McDonald (2011) introduced finance-tuned dictionaries; Hansen and McMahon (2016) showed FOMC language contains separable "action" and "patience" signals that markets price. The tone paradigm, however, inherits two weaknesses this paper documents: it scores the statement's *level*, not the market-priced *change* (the taper-tantrum exhibit in Section 3.5), and — our Section 7 result — its resolution collapses precisely on the paradigm-shift statements that define modern communication. The LLM-based diagnostic here follows the recent move to statement-level language models, with the discipline that the diagnostic must pass a five-layer audit and cross-vendor committee confirmation before it can flag anything.

**Fund flows and returns.** The aggregate-flow literature (Warther, 1995) and the flow-return feedback literature establish that flows respond to returns with a lag and carry information about demand pressure. The fund-flow reaction to monetary policy specifically is thinly documented — most work stops at prices, or at survey portfolios. We provide the class-level flow response to a decomposed policy shock, with a null (words do not move monthly flows) and a finding (risk-ordered de-risking on information) that together locate the portfolio margin of monetary-policy communication.

**Position.** None of the three lines supplies what the paper's mechanism requires: a decomposition that treats the statement as simultaneous action-and-information, a portfolio margin measured at the monthly horizon, and a measurement layer audited against its own failure modes. The assembly is the contribution.
